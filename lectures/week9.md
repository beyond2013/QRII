# Linear and Exponential Growth and Decay Models

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lecture, students should be able to:

1. Explain the concepts of growth and decay using everyday examples.
2. Understand the difference between linear and exponential growth.
3. Identify situations where linear or exponential models are appropriate.
4. Construct simple linear growth and decay models.
5. Construct simple exponential growth and decay models.
6. Calculate future values using linear and exponential formulas.
7. Interpret growth and decay graphs.
8. Apply growth and decay models to practical situations involving population, finance, business and technology.

---

## 2. Introduction: Understanding Growth and Decay

Consider the following situations:

- The population of a city increases every year.
- The price of essential commodities increases over time.
- The value of a mobile phone decreases as it gets older.
- Money deposited in a bank earns interest.
- A bacteria population doubles every few hours.
- The number of users of a new mobile application increases rapidly.

All these situations involve change over time.

Some quantities increase. This is called **growth**.

Some quantities decrease. This is called **decay**.

However, there is an important question:

**Do all quantities grow or decrease in the same way?**

Consider two examples.

### Example A: Fixed Increase

A student's monthly pocket money is Rs. 5,000.

His parents increase it by Rs. 500 every month.

| Month | Pocket Money |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 5,000 |
| 2 | 5,500 |
| 3 | 6,000 |
| 4 | 6,500 |
| 5 | 7,000 |

Notice that the amount increases by exactly Rs. 500 every month.

This is an example of linear growth.

### Example B: Percentage Increase

Suppose a student's savings are Rs. 10,000 and increase by 10% every year.

| Year | Savings |
|---|---:|
| 0 | 10,000 |
| 1 | 11,000 |
| 2 | 12,100 |
| 3 | 13,310 |
| 4 | 14,641 |

Notice that the increase is not fixed.

The amount increases by 10% of the current balance each year.

This is an example of exponential growth.

### Important Observation

There are two fundamentally different ways in which quantities can grow:

1. By adding a fixed amount.
2. By increasing by a fixed percentage.

Understanding this difference is the foundation of today's lecture.

# PART A: LINEAR GROWTH AND DECAY

## 3. What Is Linear Growth?

Linear growth occurs when a quantity increases by the same fixed amount during every equal time interval.

For example:

A person's salary increases by Rs. 2,000 every year.

| Year | Salary |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 30,000 |
| 2 | 32,000 |
| 3 | 34,000 |
| 4 | 36,000 |
| 5 | 38,000 |

The increase is always Rs. 2,000.

This is linear growth.

### General Linear Growth Formula

A linear growth model can be written as:

**y = a + bt**

Where:

- y = Final value.
- a = Initial value.
- b = Fixed amount of increase per time period.
- t = Number of time periods.

### Example

Suppose a person's salary is initially Rs. 30,000 and increases by Rs. 2,000 every year.

The mathematical model is:

S(t) = 30000 + 2000t

Where:

- S(t) = Salary after t years.
- 30000 = Initial salary.
- 2000 = Annual increase.
- t = Number of years.

Find the salary after 5 years.

S(5) = 30000 + 2000(5)

S(5) = 30000 + 10000

S(5) = Rs. 40,000

Therefore, the salary after 5 years will be Rs. 40,000.

### Key Point

In linear growth:

**The amount of increase remains constant.**

---

## 4. What Is Linear Decay?

Linear decay occurs when a quantity decreases by the same fixed amount during every equal time interval.

Consider a mobile phone purchased for Rs. 100,000.

Suppose its value decreases by Rs. 10,000 every year.

| Year | Mobile Phone Value |
|---|---:|
| 0 | 100,000 |
| 1 | 90,000 |
| 2 | 80,000 |
| 3 | 70,000 |
| 4 | 60,000 |
| 5 | 50,000 |

The value decreases by Rs. 10,000 every year.

This is linear decay.

### General Linear Decay Formula

**y = a - bt**

Where:

- y = Remaining value.
- a = Initial value.
- b = Fixed amount of decrease per time period.
- t = Number of time periods.

### Example

A laptop costs Rs. 120,000 and loses Rs. 15,000 in value every year.

The model is:

V(t) = 120000 - 15000t

Find its value after 4 years.

V(4) = 120000 - 15000(4)

V(4) = 120000 - 60000

V(4) = Rs. 60,000

Therefore, the laptop's estimated value after 4 years is Rs. 60,000.

### Key Point

In linear decay:

**The amount of decrease remains constant.**

---

## 5. Graphs of Linear Growth and Decay

Linear growth and decay are represented by straight lines.

### Linear Growth

Example:

y = 10 + 2t

As t increases, y increases at a constant rate.

The graph moves upward from left to right.

### Linear Decay

Example:

y = 100 - 5t

As t increases, y decreases at a constant rate.

The graph moves downward from left to right.

### Important Observations

| Feature | Linear Growth | Linear Decay |
|---|---|---|
| Initial value | Positive or zero | Positive or zero |
| Change | Fixed increase | Fixed decrease |
| Graph | Upward straight line | Downward straight line |
| Rate of change | Constant positive | Constant negative |

### Check Your Understanding

Suppose a water tank contains 1,000 litres of water.

Water is being removed at a constant rate of 100 litres per hour.

Ask students:

1. How much water remains after one hour?
2. How much water remains after three hours?
3. How much water remains after five hours?
4. After how many hours will the tank become empty?

Model:

W(t) = 1000 - 100t

Students should be able to answer these questions using the model.

# PART B: EXPONENTIAL GROWTH AND DECAY

## 6. What Is Exponential Growth?

Exponential growth occurs when a quantity increases by a fixed percentage during every equal time interval.

Unlike linear growth, the amount of increase is not fixed.

Instead, the increase depends on the current value.

Consider an investment of Rs. 10,000 that increases by 10% every year.

### Year 0

Initial amount = Rs. 10,000

### Year 1

10% of 10,000 = 1,000

New amount = 10,000 + 1,000

= Rs. 11,000

### Year 2

10% of 11,000 = 1,100

New amount = 11,000 + 1,100

= Rs. 12,100

### Year 3

10% of 12,100 = 1,210

New amount = 12,100 + 1,210

= Rs. 13,310

Notice something important:

The increase becomes larger every year.

Why?

Because the percentage is calculated on the new amount, not the original amount.

This is exponential growth.

### General Exponential Growth Formula

**y = a(1 + r)^t**

Where:

- y = Final value.
- a = Initial value.
- r = Growth rate expressed as a decimal.
- t = Number of time periods.

For example:

10% = 10/100 = 0.10

5% = 5/100 = 0.05

20% = 20/100 = 0.20

### Solved Example

A town has an initial population of 50,000.

Its population increases by 5% every year.

Find the population after 3 years.

Given:

a = 50,000

r = 0.05

t = 3

Formula:

P(t) = a(1 + r)^t

Substitute:

P(3) = 50000(1 + 0.05)^3

P(3) = 50000(1.05)^3

P(3) = 50000 × 1.157625

P(3) = 57,881.25

Therefore, the estimated population after 3 years is approximately 57,881 people.

### Key Point

In exponential growth:

**The percentage increase remains constant, but the actual amount of increase becomes larger.**

---

## 7. What Is Exponential Decay?

Exponential decay occurs when a quantity decreases by a fixed percentage during every equal time interval.

Consider a mobile phone purchased for Rs. 100,000.

Suppose its value decreases by 20% every year.

### Year 0

Value = Rs. 100,000

### Year 1

20% of 100,000 = 20,000

Remaining value:

100,000 - 20,000 = Rs. 80,000

### Year 2

20% of 80,000 = 16,000

Remaining value:

80,000 - 16,000 = Rs. 64,000

### Year 3

20% of 64,000 = 12,800

Remaining value:

64,000 - 12,800 = Rs. 51,200

Notice that the actual amount of decrease becomes smaller each year.

However, the percentage decrease remains 20%.

This is exponential decay.

### General Exponential Decay Formula

**y = a(1 - r)^t**

Where:

- y = Remaining value.
- a = Initial value.
- r = Decay rate expressed as a decimal.
- t = Number of time periods.

### Solved Example

A laptop costs Rs. 150,000.

Its value decreases by 10% every year.

Find its estimated value after 3 years.

Given:

a = 150,000

r = 0.10

t = 3

Formula:

V(t) = a(1 - r)^t

Substitute:

V(3) = 150000(1 - 0.10)^3

V(3) = 150000(0.90)^3

V(3) = 150000 × 0.729

V(3) = Rs. 109,350

Therefore, the estimated value after 3 years is Rs. 109,350.

### Key Point

In exponential decay:

**The percentage decrease remains constant, but the actual amount of decrease becomes smaller.**

## 8. Comparing Linear and Exponential Models

This is one of the most important concepts of this lecture.

Consider two investments.

Both start with Rs. 10,000.

### Investment A: Linear Growth

It increases by Rs. 1,000 every year.

Model:

A(t) = 10000 + 1000t

### Investment B: Exponential Growth

It increases by 10% every year.

Model:

B(t) = 10000(1.10)^t

Let's compare them.

| Year | Linear Growth | Exponential Growth |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0 | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| 1 | 11,000 | 11,000 |
| 2 | 12,000 | 12,100 |
| 3 | 13,000 | 13,310 |
| 4 | 14,000 | 14,641 |
| 5 | 15,000 | 16,105 |
| 6 | 16,000 | 17,716 |

Notice:

- Initially, both investments have the same value.
- Linear growth adds the same amount every year.
- Exponential growth adds a percentage of the current value.
- Over time, exponential growth becomes faster.

### Why Does Exponential Growth Become Faster?

Because the growth is calculated on an increasing value.

For example:

10% of 10,000 = 1,000

10% of 20,000 = 2,000

10% of 30,000 = 3,000

The same percentage produces increasingly larger additions.

### Important Lesson

**Linear growth adds. Exponential growth multiplies.**

This is perhaps the simplest way to remember the difference.

## 9. Applications of Linear Growth and Decay

Linear models are useful when quantities change by approximately the same amount during equal time intervals.

Some practical examples include:

### 1. Salary Increases

A salary increases by Rs. 3,000 every year.

Model:

S(t) = S₀ + 3000t

### 2. Water Consumption

A tank loses 50 litres of water every hour.

Model:

W(t) = W₀ - 50t

### 3. Transportation Cost

A taxi charges Rs. 200 as a fixed starting fee and Rs. 50 per kilometre.

Model:

C(x) = 200 + 50x

### 4. Depreciation by Fixed Amount

A machine loses Rs. 20,000 in value every year.

Model:

V(t) = V₀ - 20000t

### 5. Monthly Savings

A student saves Rs. 2,000 every month.

Model:

S(t) = S₀ + 2000t

## 10. Applications of Exponential Growth and Decay

Exponential models are useful when a quantity changes by a constant percentage or proportional rate.

### 1. Compound Interest

Money deposited in a bank earns interest.

If the interest is compounded annually:

A(t) = P(1 + r)^t

Where:

- P = Initial investment.
- r = Annual interest rate.
- t = Number of years.

Example:

Rs. 20,000 is deposited at 5% annual compound interest.

Find the amount after 2 years.

A(2) = 20000(1.05)^2

A(2) = 20000 × 1.1025

A(2) = Rs. 22,050

### 2. Population Growth

Population may grow by a percentage every year.

Model:

P(t) = P₀(1 + r)^t

### 3. Bacterial Growth

Suppose a bacterial population doubles every hour.

| Hour | Number of Bacteria |
|---|---:|
| 0 | 100 |
| 1 | 200 |
| 2 | 400 |
| 3 | 800 |
| 4 | 1,600 |

Model:

B(t) = 100 × 2^t

This is exponential growth.

### 4. Depreciation

The value of a computer or vehicle often decreases by a fixed percentage every year.

Model:

V(t) = V₀(1 - r)^t

### 5. Medicine Elimination

Some medicines decrease in concentration in the body over time.

A simplified model may describe this as exponential decay.

### 6. Technology Adoption

The number of users adopting a new technology may initially grow rapidly.

For example:

- Mobile applications.
- Social media platforms.
- Online payment systems.
- Digital learning platforms.

Exponential growth can sometimes approximate the early stages of adoption, although real-world adoption eventually slows as the market becomes saturated.

## 11. Understanding Percentage and Decimal Conversion

Students often make mistakes when converting percentages into decimal values.

Remember:

Percentage means per hundred.

To convert a percentage into a decimal, divide it by 100.

| Percentage | Decimal |
|---|---:|
| 2% | 0.02 |
| 5% | 0.05 |
| 10% | 0.10 |
| 15% | 0.15 |
| 20% | 0.20 |
| 25% | 0.25 |
| 50% | 0.50 |
| 100% | 1.00 |

### Practice

Convert the following percentages into decimals:

1. 8%
2. 12%
3. 18%
4. 30%
5. 45%

Remember:

For 15% growth:

1 + 0.15 = 1.15

For 15% decay:

1 - 0.15 = 0.85

This is why the exponential formulas use:

Growth: (1 + r)

Decay: (1 - r)

## 12. A Simple Way to Identify the Correct Model

Suppose you are given a real-world problem.

How do you decide whether to use a linear or exponential model?

Ask yourself:

**Question 1: Does the quantity increase or decrease by a fixed amount?**

If yes, a linear model may be appropriate.

Example:

A salary increases by Rs. 2,000 every year.

**Question 2: Does the quantity increase or decrease by a fixed percentage?**

If yes, an exponential model may be appropriate.

Example:

A bank deposit earns 10% interest annually.

### Comparison Table

| Feature | Linear Model | Exponential Model |
|---|---|---|
| Type of change | Fixed amount | Fixed percentage |
| Mathematical operation | Addition or subtraction | Multiplication |
| Growth formula | a + bt | a(1 + r)^t |
| Decay formula | a - bt | a(1 - r)^t |
| Graph | Straight line | Curved line |
| Growth pattern | Constant increase | Increasing increase |
| Decay pattern | Constant decrease | Decreasing decrease |

### A Useful Classroom Cue

Ask students:

"If I give you Rs. 1,000 every month, is that linear or exponential growth?"

Answer: Linear growth.

"If I increase your monthly allowance by 10% every month, is that linear or exponential growth?"

Answer: Exponential growth.

This simple comparison helps students remember the difference.

## 13. Introduction to Continuous Exponential Growth and Decay

So far, we have discussed exponential changes occurring at regular intervals, such as annually or monthly.

However, some processes change continuously.

For such situations, we use the exponential function:

**y = ae^(kt)**

Where:

- a = Initial value.
- e = Mathematical constant approximately equal to 2.71828.
- k = Continuous growth or decay rate.
- t = Time.

If k is positive, the quantity grows.

If k is negative, the quantity decays.

For example:

P(t) = 1000e^(0.05t)

This represents continuous exponential growth.

And:

P(t) = 1000e^(-0.05t)

This represents continuous exponential decay.

### Important Note

The number e is a special mathematical constant used in many areas of mathematics, science and computing.

For this introductory lecture, students only need to recognize the formula and understand that it represents continuous exponential growth or decay.

Detailed calculations involving e are not necessary at this stage.

# 16. Key Takeaways

Let's summarize the most important concepts.

1. Growth means an increase in quantity over time.
2. Decay means a decrease in quantity over time.
3. Linear growth occurs when a quantity increases by a fixed amount during equal time intervals.
4. Linear decay occurs when a quantity decreases by a fixed amount during equal time intervals.
5. Exponential growth occurs when a quantity increases by a fixed percentage during equal time intervals.
6. Exponential decay occurs when a quantity decreases by a fixed percentage during equal time intervals.
7. Linear models are represented by straight lines.
8. Exponential models are represented by curves.
9. Compound interest, population growth and bacterial growth are examples of exponential growth.
10. Depreciation and medicine elimination can be modeled using exponential decay.
11. Mathematical models help us understand and predict how quantities change over time.

## 17. Final Reflection

Consider the following questions:

- Why does money deposited at compound interest grow faster over time?
- Why can a population increase very rapidly under favorable conditions?
- Why does a mobile phone lose value as it gets older?
- Why does a 10% increase in the price of an expensive item represent more money than a 10% increase in a cheaper item?
- Why is it important to distinguish between fixed increases and percentage increases?

These questions demonstrate that growth and decay are not just mathematical concepts. They describe many processes in our daily lives.

**The central idea of this lecture is simple:**

Linear models describe change by adding or subtracting a fixed amount.

Exponential models describe change by multiplying by a fixed factor.

Understanding this difference allows us to select appropriate mathematical models for real-world problems.
